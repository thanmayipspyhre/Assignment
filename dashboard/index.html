SupeRR Selector Season 2 · Round 1

Who delivers when the match is on the line?

We rated every IPL and T20I batter and bowler since 2022 on the balls where the result could swing, and compared each with their own usual level. The result is two ranked top-10 lists, built only from the supplied data.

Method

How pressure is calculated

Pressure belongs to the match situation, not the player. A ball counts as a pressure ball only if all three tests pass, using information available before the ball is bowled.

  1. 1

    Past the powerplay

    At least 36 legal balls have been bowled. Early overs look high-stakes to the model only because it is uncertain there, so they are excluded.

  2. 2

    High leverage

    The state is in the top 25% of its innings for leverage: how much the win probability usually swings on a ball in a situation like this.

  3. 3

    Contest still open

    The batting side's win probability is between 20% and 80%. A late over in a decided match has no real pressure.

15.3% of all balls pass all three tests: 29,392 of 192,466 deliveries.

Conditions for a batter

  • Faces a legal delivery (wides and no-balls are not held against the batter)
  • At least 36 legal balls of the innings bowled
  • State in the top 25% of leverage for the innings
  • Batting side's win probability between 20% and 80%

Judged on: runs above the league average in the same situation, less 5.5 runs for their own dismissal, compared with their usual level. Eligible with 100+ pressure balls, 8+ matches, 120+ ordinary balls.

Conditions for a bowler

  • Any delivery counts, including wides and no-balls (extras under pressure count against the bowler)
  • At least 36 legal balls of the innings bowled
  • State in the top 25% of leverage for the innings
  • Batting side's win probability between 20% and 80%

Judged on: runs saved against the league average in the same situation, plus 5.5 runs per wicket (run-outs excluded), compared with their usual level. Eligible with 100+ pressure deliveries, 8+ matches, 120+ ordinary deliveries.

A pressure ball moves the match about three times as much, and wickets are more likely. Batters do not slow down; they take more risk to keep scoring, which is why dismissal cost is part of the rating.

The win-probability model

Gradient-boosted trees on the state before each ball

  • Inputs: innings, balls left, wickets lost, runs, run rates, and for chases the target and required rate.
  • Every prediction is out-of-fold, so no ball is scored by a model that saw its own match.
  • AUC 0.81 overall and 0.90 in chases; weaker in the first innings (0.69) where there is no target.

Trust

How reliable is this?

The win-probability model is accurate. The player ratings are only moderately reliable, because each player has between 100 and 470 pressure balls. The set of names is more trustworthy than the order within it.

Known limits

  • Small samples. Rank ranges are wide, and a few picks have fewer than 20 matches behind them.
  • The 100-ball minimum was chosen after seeing the results. It was raised from 60 to remove picks that rested on usual level alone.
  • No pitch or venue model. The data has a venue but no conditions; most of the 98 venues have too few matches.
  • No opposition adjustment. T20I includes weaker sides than the IPL; IPL-only and T20I-only checks are shown on each card.
  • Batting and bowling rated separately, as the task asks. Fielding is not credited.
  • Batters' usual level predicts nearly as well as the pressure rating on held-out matches (0.125 against 0.085).
